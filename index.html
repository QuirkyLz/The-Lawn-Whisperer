Reading your lawn — early warning signs
Yellowing
  • Even, pale yellow across the lawn — usually nitrogen hungry, due a feed.
  • Yellow with green veins — often iron deficiency, especially after rain leaches the soil.
Thinning or bare patches
  • Circular dead patches expanding outward — check for fungal disease.
  • Ragged, chewed leaf blades and birds pecking at the lawn — check for grubs or caterpillars underneath.
Dry, crispy patches that won't green up
  • Often hydrophobic soil — water is running off instead of soaking in. A wetting agent is the fix, not more water.
Lawn armyworm & caterpillar watch

Armyworm moths lay eggs that hatch into caterpillars which strip grass blades overnight — a healthy lawn can look chewed and brown within days, usually worst from late summer into autumn.

Signs
  • Ragged, notched leaf blades and patches that look "scalped" overnight.
  • Increased bird activity on the lawn early morning — they're hunting the caterpillars.
  • Small green or brown caterpillars visible if you part the grass at dusk.
What to reach for
  • A fast knockdown liquid insecticide once you see damage.
  • A residual granular like Acelepryn applied preventatively in early summer if armyworm has hit your area before.
Weed control — pre-emergent vs post-emergent

Pre-emergent herbicides (like Barricade, Spartan or Onset) form a barrier in the soil that stops weed seeds germinating — apply before the weed season starts, not after weeds are already up.

Post-emergent, selective herbicides (like NSFW or Monument) are sprayed onto weeds that are already growing and target the weed without harming the lawn — always check the label lists your turf type as safe.

Don't apply weed control on a lawn under stress (drought, recent scalping, brand-new turf) — wait until it's actively growing and established.

Fungal disease — when to treat

Most lawn fungus shows up in warm, humid, still conditions. Look for rings or patches of discoloured grass, sometimes with a smoke-grey or orange tinge at the edge.

  • Brown patch / large circular rings — a broad-spectrum fungicide like Heritage Maxx or Bumper.
  • Damping off or root rot in cool, wet conditions — a systemic fungicide like Subdue Maxx targets Pythium at the root.

Improve airflow and avoid watering in the evening before reaching for a spray.

Growth regulators — do you actually need one?

A plant growth regulator (PGR) like Primo Maxx doesn't feed the lawn — it slows vertical leaf growth so the plant pushes energy sideways into density and root mass instead. Most home lawns don't need one until the turf is fully established.

Trimmit is a lighter-touch option often used along garden bed edges and turf aprons.

Glossary — decoding the label
Foliar
Applied to and absorbed through the leaf — fast acting, shorter-lived than granular.
Granular
Solid prill spread on soil, broken down over weeks — slower, longer-lasting.
Slow/controlled-release
Nutrients feed out gradually over the stated period instead of dumping all at once.
Wetting agent / surfactant
Breaks water's surface tension so it soaks into hydrophobic soil instead of running off.
Soil penetrant
Helps water and dissolved nutrients move deeper into the root zone, faster than a standard wetting agent.
Pre-emergent
Stops weed seeds germinating — applied before weeds appear.
Post-emergent
Kills weeds that are already growing.
Selective
Kills the target weed without harming the lawn species listed on the label.
Systemic
Taken up into the plant's tissue rather than sitting on the surface — longer lasting control.
Rhizome / stolon
Underground and above-ground runners a lawn spreads and repairs itself with.
Thatch
A layer of dead organic matter between soil and green leaf — too much blocks water and feed.
PGR
Plant growth regulator — slows vertical growth, redirects energy to density and roots.
